Finance Internship Project Ideas: 7 Practical Equity & Financial Analysis Topics

Struggling to choose a topic for your college finance project or equity research training report? Here are seven structured, data-driven project frameworks using real Indian company filings.

By Harion Research · Updated 2026-10-31 · 9 min read

What separates a strong finance project from a weak one

Every year, thousands of B.Com, BBA, BMS, and MBA Finance students in India must submit a summer internship report or capstone finance project. Too often, students pick overly broad topics like 'A Study on Mutual Funds in India' or 'Overview of the Indian Banking Sector' and fill 50 pages with copied definitions and pie charts from secondary websites.

Faculty examiners and industry interviewers immediately recognize when a project lacks original analytical work. A strong finance internship project is narrow, specific, and grounded in primary financial data. Instead of writing about an entire economy in generalities, you select a specific listed company or a tight cohort of three to five peer companies on the NSE or BSE, extract five years of audited financial statements, and test a clear analytical question.

Below are seven concrete finance internship project ideas that you can execute using public annual reports and the Harion Market Analysis Learning Lab (Research Scanner).

Project Idea 1: DuPont ROE & ROCE Decomposition Across Sector Peers

Pick three to four listed companies within a single Indian industry—for example, specialty chemicals, auto ancillaries, or fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG). Extract five years of income statements and balance sheets for each company.

Break down Return on Equity (ROE) into its three DuPont components: Net Profit Margin (operating efficiency), Asset Turnover (capital utilization speed), and Financial Leverage (total assets divided by shareholders' equity). Perform a parallel decomposition on Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) using EBIT margin and capital-employed turnover.

Your project report should explain why two companies in the same industry achieve similar headline ROE through completely different paths—for instance, why Company A relies on high brand margins and zero debt while Company B relies on thin margins, rapid inventory turnover, and higher borrowings.

Project Idea 2: Working Capital Cycle & Cash Conversion Study

In capital-intensive sectors such as industrial engineering, building materials, cables and wires, or pharmaceuticals, revenue growth on the income statement does not always translate into cash in the bank. For this project, select a mid-cap or large-cap manufacturing company from the NIFTY 500 universe in the Harion Research Scanner.

Calculate Inventory Days, Trade Receivable Days, and Trade Payable Days across a 5-to-7-year period. Compare the resulting Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) against the ratio of Cash Flow from Operations (CFO) to EBITDA. Investigate the Notes to Accounts to see how raw-material price swings, dealer credit terms, or capacity expansions affected free cash flow generation over the cycle.

Project Idea 3: Capital Allocation & Capex Effectiveness Analysis

When a profitable business generates operating cash flow, management has five choices for deploying that capital: reinvesting in organic capital expenditure (capex), funding working capital, paying down debt, acquiring other businesses, or distributing cash to shareholders via dividends and buybacks.

Track 10 years of Cash Flow Statements for a listed Indian company. Sum total cumulative CFO over the decade and map exactly what percentage went to capex, acquisitions, debt service, and dividends. Then calculate Incremental Return on Capital Employed (the change in EBIT divided by the change in capital employed over multi-year blocks) to evaluate whether management's capital expenditure projects created or diluted economic value.

Project Idea 4: Balance-Sheet Leverage & Interest Coverage Stress Test

Select a cyclical sector such as metals, cement, hospitality, or commercial vehicles. Using the Financial Ratio Study module in the Research Scanner, compare Debt-to-Equity and Interest Coverage ratios across five listed peers.

Go beyond single-year ratios by reading the debt-maturity schedule and borrowing footnotes in each company's annual report. Build a simple sensitivity table in your spreadsheet showing what happens to each company's Interest Coverage Ratio (EBIT divided by Finance Costs) if sector revenue drops by 15% and operating margins compress by 300 basis points—demonstrating risk-first thinking in practice.

Project Idea 5: Revenue Mix & Unit Economics in Indian IT or Healthcare

Service-oriented sectors such as IT services, diagnostic chains, and hospital networks disclose rich operational metrics in their investor presentations and annual reports. For a diagnostic chain or hospital group, track Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed (ARPOB), occupancy rates, average length of stay (ALOS), and brownfield vs. greenfield bed additions. For an IT services company, track client bucket progression, attrition, utilization, and offshore revenue mix. Connect these non-GAAP operating metrics directly to reported operating margins and ROCE.

Project Idea 6: Multi-Factor Price, Volume & Fundamental Regime Study

Using the Market Analysis Learning Lab's historical observation frameworks, study how a cohort of NIFTY 200 companies behaved across distinct market phases (trending, consolidation, and high-volatility phases) on delayed End-of-Day data. Pair these historical price-structure and relative-volume observations with underlying quarterly earnings and margin trends from company filings to document how fundamental operating changes and market structure interact over multi-quarter windows.

Project Idea 7: A Complete Single-Company Equity Research Case Study

The most comprehensive project—and the exact deliverable of the 60-day Harion Equity Research Internship Program—is a full-length educational equity research report on a single listed company. Combining all of the modules above, you screen the universe to select your company, spread five years of financial statements, analyze industry competitiveness, build explicit valuation sensitivity tables, and document key risks under weekly mentor review.
