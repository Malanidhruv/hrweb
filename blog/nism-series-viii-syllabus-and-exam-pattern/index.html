NISM Series VIII: Syllabus, Exam Pattern, Passing Marks and 30-Day Study Plan

Everything students and finance candidates need to know about the NISM-Series-VIII Equity Derivatives Certification Examination: exam structure, negative marking rules, core topics and a 30-day preparation plan.

By Harion Research · Updated 2026-10-31 · 8 min read

What is the NISM Series VIII Equity Derivatives Certification?

The NISM-Series-VIII: Equity Derivatives Certification Examination is an official certification administered by the National Institute of Securities Markets (NISM) under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) certification regulations. It is designed to establish a common minimum knowledge benchmark for associated persons functioning as approved users and sales personnel of trading members of an equity derivatives exchange.

For commerce, finance, and MBA students in India, passing NISM Series VIII is a widely recognized credential when applying for roles in capital markets operations, institutional and retail broking desks, wealth management, risk management, and derivatives dealing.

Exam pattern, duration, passing marks and negative marking

Understanding the exact test mechanics is critical before you begin studying, especially because of the negative marking rule:

- Total Questions: 100 multiple-choice questions.

- Maximum Marks: 100 marks (1 mark per question).

- Exam Duration: 120 minutes (2 hours).

- Passing Score: 60% (60 marks out of 100).

- Negative Marking: Yes—25% of the marks assigned to a question (0.25 marks deducted for every wrong answer).

- Certificate Validity: 3 years from the date of passing.

- Exam Fee: NISM typically lists the test fee at ₹1,500 plus applicable taxes/charges; candidates should always verify the latest fee and slot availability directly on the official NISM portal (nism.ac.in) before registering.

Complete NISM Series VIII syllabus breakdown

Questions on the exam are drawn directly from the official NISM Equity Derivatives workbook across ten core subject areas:

1. Basics of Derivatives: Definition of derivatives, history of derivatives markets, Indian derivatives market structure, market participants (hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs), and OTC vs. exchange-traded contracts.

2. Understanding Index: How stock market indices (such as NIFTY 50 and SENSEX) are constructed—free-float market capitalization weighting, price weighting, impact cost, and index management.

3. Introduction to Forwards and Futures: Forward vs. futures contracts, contract specifications (lot size, tick size, expiry cycle, open interest, volume), payoff charts for long and short futures.

4. Pricing of Futures & Hedging: The cost-of-carry model, concept of basis, contango and backwardation, cash-and-carry arbitrage, and using index/stock futures to hedge equity portfolios (including beta adjustment).

5. Introduction to Options & Option Pricing: Call and put options, American vs. European style, moneyness (ITM, ATM, OTM), intrinsic value vs. time value, put-call parity, Determinants of option prices (spot price, strike, volatility, time to expiry, interest rates, dividends), and the Option Greeks (Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega, Rho).

6. Option Trading Strategies: Payoff profiles, breakeven points, maximum gain and maximum loss calculations for protective puts, covered calls, bull/bear spreads, straddles, and strangles.

7. Trading Mechanism: Order types (market, limit, stop-loss, IOC), corporate action adjustments on lots and strikes.

8. Introduction to Clearing and Settlement System: Role of the Clearing Corporation, clearing members vs. trading members, daily mark-to-market (MTM) settlement, final settlement, physical delivery vs. cash settlement, and risk containment measures (Initial Margin, SPAN Margin, Exposure Margin, VaR, liquid net worth).

9. Legal and Regulatory Environment: Relevant provisions of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act (SCRA), SEBI Act, position limits for clients/trading members/FPIs, and surveillance.

10. Accounting, Taxation & Sales Practices: Basic accounting treatment of futures and options, taxation of derivatives transactions as business income, client onboarding (KYC, Risk Disclosure Document), and investor grievance redressal.

Why candidates fail—and how to handle the 25% negative marking

The most common reason candidates miss the 60% mark by 2 or 3 points is blind guessing. If a candidate knows 56 questions accurately and guesses randomly on the remaining 44 questions, statistical probability yields roughly 11 right answers (+11 marks) and 33 wrong answers (-8.25 marks), leaving them right on the razor's edge—or below 60 if luck runs against them.

Equally important are numerical payoff and margin questions. The exam regularly tests numerical problems on futures hedging using portfolio beta, option spread breakeven points, mark-to-market daily cash flows, and liquid net worth after margin deductions. Practicing these calculations by hand is essential.

A structured 30-day preparation plan

Week 1 (Units 1–4): Read the official NISM workbook chapters on derivatives basics, index construction, impact cost, and futures pricing/hedging. Practice all end-of-chapter numerical problems on cost-of-carry and beta hedging.

Week 2 (Units 5–6): Master options fundamentals, the Greeks, and option strategy payoffs. Draw payoff diagrams and calculate maximum loss/gain and breakeven points for every standard spread without looking at formulas.

Week 3 (Units 7–10): Study trading mechanisms, clearing and settlement (memorize liquid net worth requirements, SPAN + Exposure margin rules, and position limits), regulatory provisions, and investor protection.

Week 4 (Full-length timed mocks & revision): Take at least three 100-question, 120-minute mock exams under strict negative-marking rules. Review every incorrect answer and skip any question on exam day where you cannot eliminate at least two options.
