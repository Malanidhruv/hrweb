01 / Data Architecture

Universe and data sources

The Harion Research Scanner covers active Indian equities listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange). To allow structured comparisons across companies of comparable scale and liquidity, the universe is segmented into eight study groups: NIFTY 50, NIFTY 200, NIFTY 500, All NSE Stocks, BSE Large Cap, BSE Mid Cap, BSE Small Cap, and All BSE Stocks.

All price and volume series in the scanner are based on delayed End-of-Day (EOD) exchange data. Fundamental ratios and financial statement line items are derived from reported quarterly and annual corporate filings. Because corporate actions (splits, bonuses, rights issues, mergers) and accounting restatements can affect historical series, participants are trained to cross-check every screened observation against official exchange disclosures and annual reports before drawing analytical conclusions.

02 / Screening Logic

How each filter family works

Screening is a method for reducing a universe of thousands of listed companies to a manageable cohort that shares specific observable characteristics. The Research Scanner groups filters into three families:

A. Price structure filters

Price filters measure where a stock trades relative to its own historical range (such as 52-week high/low proximity or multi-week consolidation ranges) and relative to standard moving averages (such as 20-day, 50-day, and 200-day simple or exponential averages). These observations help learners classify whether a security has been in a trending, sideways, or volatile regime over a chosen historical lookback window.

B. Volume & participation filters

Volume filters compare daily traded turnover against rolling historical averages (e.g., 20-day average volume). Observing price changes alongside relative volume teaches students how institutional and market-wide participation differs between quiet consolidation periods and high-activity disclosures such as quarterly earnings releases.

03 / Company Research Process

Fundamental analysis workflow

A screen never replaces reading the filings. In the Equity Research Internship Program, once a company is shortlisted in the scanner, analysts follow a four-step fundamental workflow:

  1. Business model & unit economics: Identify what the company manufactures or provides, revenue breakdown by segment and geography, customer concentration, and raw-material or employee cost structure from the Annual Report and Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A).
  2. Accounting quality & cash conversion: Compare cumulative EBITDA and Net Profit over 5–10 years against cumulative Cash Flow from Operations (CFO). Examine receivable days, inventory days, related-party transactions, auditor remarks, and contingent liabilities.
  3. Capital allocation track record: Study how management has deployed incremental capital—organic capex, working capital, debt reduction, acquisitions, or dividends—and whether incremental Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) exceeds the cost of capital.
  4. Sector & competitive benchmarking: Compare margins, asset turnover, and leverage against 3–5 listed domestic peers to understand whether performance is driven by company-specific execution or an industry-wide cycle.
04 / Valuation Discipline

Valuation approach

We teach valuation as an exercise in making assumptions explicit, not as a formula for generating a "target price". Interns study two complementary lenses:

  • Intrinsic cash-flow logic (DCF sensitivity): Projecting free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) or equity (FCFE) across explicit operating scenarios (base, conservative, stress) to see what revenue growth and operating margin assumptions are implied by the current market capitalization.
  • Relative multiple context: Comparing P/E, P/B, and EV/EBITDA across a peer group and against the company's own 5-year and 10-year historical median multiples, adjusted for differences in ROE, ROCE, leverage, and earnings cyclicality.

In all Harion learning reports, valuation sections conclude by stating the sensitivity of estimates to key variables (discount rate, terminal growth, margin normalization) rather than issuing buy, sell, or hold ratings.

05 / Boundaries & Risk Disclosure

Limitations and what the scanner is not

Every quantitative and fundamental tool has structural limitations that analysts must keep in view:

  • Not an advisory or signal engine: The Research Scanner does not predict future stock prices, generate buy/sell calls, or rank "top stocks".
  • Backward-looking accounting data: Reported financial ratios reflect past fiscal periods. An industry downturn, regulatory change, or balance-sheet event occurring after the latest filing date will not appear in trailing ratios until reported.
  • Accounting heterogeneity: Different sectors (for example, banking and NBFCs versus manufacturing) use distinct financial statement formats; ratios such as EV/EBITDA or Debt-to-Equity are not meaningful for banks and financial institutions.
  • Data latency and reporting errors: Automated feeds can misclassify one-time exceptional items or corporate actions. Always verify numbers against audited company filings on NSE/BSE.
